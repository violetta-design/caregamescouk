♥ My Journey: From Family Carer to Creating Care Games Co

Why caring became my life, long before it became my profession.

Caring Before It Had a Name

People often ask me why I became a carer.

The truth is, I don't remember making that decision. Caring became part of my life long before anyone called it a profession.

I was born in Romania and later moved to Greece, where I built my family and raised my twin daughters. Long before I ever worked in care, I was looking after the people closest to me. My mother's health declined first, then my father needed my support until he passed away. Later, I cared for my father-in-law and then my mother-in-law, all while raising my daughters.

Looking back, caring wasn't something I chose as a career. It was simply how I learned to express love, responsibility and respect for the people who depended on me.

When my daughters became independent, I wanted a fresh start. I moved to the United Kingdom on my own, completed my training, and became a professional live-in carer.

For the first time, something I had done all my life finally had a professional title.

What Live-In Care Really Means

Over the years, I discovered that caring is about far more than helping someone wash, dress or prepare a meal.

The real responsibility is protecting someone's dignity when they are at their most vulnerable. It is learning to read the silence when someone cannot explain how they feel. It is becoming the calm person in the room when everything around them feels uncertain.

Live-in care is especially personal. You do not simply visit for a few hours; you become part of someone's everyday life. You celebrate birthdays together, share cups of tea, watch the news, enjoy quiet conversations, and sometimes sit together in silence because that is exactly what is needed.

The Small Moments That Make It Worth It

The emotional side of caring never becomes easy. Watching people lose memories, independence or confidence is heartbreaking.

Yet it is also the most rewarding profession I have ever known.

A smile after a difficult day. A familiar song that suddenly unlocks a memory. A laugh neither of you expected. Those small moments remind me why this work matters.

I wish carers were recognised not only for the practical support they provide but as professionals who build trust, preserve dignity and help people continue living meaningful lives.

Good care is not simply about completing tasks. It is about patience, communication, observation, creativity and compassion.

Carers often become the bridge between a person, their family and the healthcare system, yet much of that work happens quietly, behind closed doors. I hope society begins to value emotional intelligence as highly as clinical skills, because both change lives.

After years of working with people living with dementia, I realised that the most meaningful moments rarely came from medication or routines.

They came from connection.

A conversation. A memory. A simple activity shared together.

I wanted to create more of those moments, and that is how Care Games Co was born.

♥ ♥ ♥

From Caring to Creating

I did not set out to build a technology company. I wanted to create practical tools that could help carers and families connect in meaningful ways.

I had no background in software development. Everything I know about building websites, digital products and AI-assisted development has been self-taught.

Artificial intelligence became my learning partner. It helped me transform ideas born from real-life caring into practical tools that other carers could use.

Today, I have built my own website, created digital dementia resources, and developed browser-based applications designed to reduce stress, encourage conversation and support both carers and families.

To me, AI is not about replacing carers. It is about giving them better tools, freeing up more time for the one thing technology can never replace: genuine human connection.

A Note to Future Carers

Never forget that people will remember how you made them feel long after they forget what you did.

Keep learning. Protect your own wellbeing. Ask for help when you need it, and never lose your curiosity.

Healthcare is changing rapidly, especially with advances in technology and artificial intelligence. But compassion will always remain the most valuable skill we can bring.

Technology should always support humanity — never replace it.

About Violetta

Violetta is a live-in carer with over ten years of experience and the founder of Care Games Co, creating dementia-friendly digital tools that help carers and families connect.
